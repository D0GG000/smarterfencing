## Initial training

As mentioned previously, the model originally had an **issue of severe overfitting**. Due to training on raw, **2d keypoints**, the model could essentially **cheat in the training process**. Rather than understanding fencer actions, it could **memorize angles** from certain videos. This led to problems a huge accuracy gap between the training and validation. While the model was near perfect on my filmed videos, it struggled with anything new.

## Second Approach

After seeing the huge discrepancy between the training and validation, I realized that I needed **camera-invariant features**. I took the raw 2d keypoints and **lifted them into 3d**. After training again on the 3d keypoints, the overfitting problem had disappeared. Still, this unveiled the truth about the accuracy of the model. The model only had around a **50% accuracy**, even though the 4 labels(chest, abdomen, arm, leg) were very different.

## A hybrid Model

The key reason that the model trained on the 3d keypoints could not converge was that **both of the fencers were in their own space**. Essentially, the model was trying to **predict where a touch landed based off only the attacker's and defender's poses**. There was **zero relative information** that could relate one fencer's movements to the other's. Essential features like distance and relative height were **destroyed by the lifting process**. Ultimately, I decided to use a **hybrid model** that could combine the best of both worlds. The **3d keypoints were used for accurate poses, while the 2d keypoints were used for relative features**. Since 2d features were prone to overfitting, they had to be **normalized by body parts of the fencers**. For example, a distance feature could be normalized by the distance between the left and right hip of a fencer. Even if the camera panned, the hip length would grow and shrink with it. In the end, this **hybrid approach worked much better**. After running inference on unseen videos (self-filmed and Youtube videos), the model could usually guess where touches landed regardless of camera angles.

## Refining the Model & Future Plans

After doing a bit more testing, I realized that I had **forgotten to include one key trait in my data**. None of the data that I had filmed included left-handed people. While my model was **pretty accurate on videos with right-handed fencers, mirroring the exact same video gave completely different results**. To combat this, I improved my model by adding mirror augmentation to the training process. This ensured that inferences done on original and mirrored videos would get similar results. In the future, I hope to **collect and train my model on more data**. Feature optimization can drastically improve a model, but a wider variety of data garuntees greater robustness on unseen videos.
