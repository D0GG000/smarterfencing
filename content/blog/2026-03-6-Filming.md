## Filming Videos 2/28/2026

On this Saturday, I went to Bida Fencing Academy to film fencing bouts. Prior to this, I was doing all testing on Youtube videos that had edited-in score lights at the bottom of the video. Once I arrived, I prepared the tripod and filmed videos with my phone camera. Standing a few meters away and with 0.5(minimum) zoom, I was able to capture the full strip and scoring lights on the side. Using this, I filmed several videos with of different fencers.

## Problems

1. After running analysis, I realized that touches were getting counted multiple times. This happened because the scoring lights for each fencer were counting both red and green color as a touch. While this worked for the solid, fake lights in Youtube videos, my phone camera picked up extra glare from every real scoring light. This glare leaked to the other side of the light box, triggering a detected touch for both fencers.

2. Even though accuracy was surprisingly good on youtube videos, it seemed to drop of the videos that I filmed. While I am not sure of the exact reason yet, this can most likely be attributed to a change in filming conditions. In my training data, touches were filmed with a greater zoom that more accurately matched Youtube videos; however, in order to capture the scoring lights, 0.5 zoom was necessary. Even though my training has features normalized by limb lengths, it is still possible that this type of zoom could alter learned ratios. Additionally, 0.5 zoom effectively lowered the resolution by a decent amount.

## Solutions & Current Progress

1. The first problem was relatively easy to address. Instead of each side accepting both red and green lights, I restricted each side to one color. I did this by using the first touch to see which side had a more solid red or green light. By comparing the solidity and brightness of the colors, I was able to filter out the glare and assign each side a seperate color for the touches after the first.

2. The second issue, being accuracy, is a more difficult problem to address. Throughout this project, accuracy is an ongoing thing that I try to improve. Nevertheless, I did still change the model architecture to be more simple. Instead of using 30 frames for inference and many 2d + 3d features, I am now only using 8 frames and 3 main features. The three features that I found most deterministic are horizontal distances from the scoring wrist to the opponent, vertical distances from the scoring wrist to the opponent, and the 3d arm angle of the scoring-arm. The theory behind this is to eliminate noise and overfitting; however, the model is still susceptible to differences in zooms and extreme changes in angle. Overall, the problem of accuracy can be tuned with two ways: changing the model or gathering a larger variety of data. The latter will be required to really see what type of model is best for this classification task.
